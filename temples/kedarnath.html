Spiritual History & Significance

Shri Kedarnath Dham stands at an altitude of 3,583 meters (11,755 ft) amidst the majestic snow-capped peaks of the Garhwal Himalayas near the headwaters of the sacred Mandakini River. It is the highest of the twelve sacred Jyotirlingas of Lord Shiva and the supreme crown of Uttarakhand's holy Chota Char Dham pilgrimage.

According to the Mahabharata, the Pandavas journeyed into the Himalayas seeking Lord Shiva to atone for the sins of the Kurukshetra war. Lord Shiva assumed the form of a celestial bull (*Nandi*), and when discovered, merged into the sacred earth, leaving His conical hump at Kedarnath as an eternal symbol of grace and forgiveness. In the 8th century, the venerated saint Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya revived the holy shrine and attained Mahasamadhi in the Himalayan shadows behind the temple.
