Spiritual History & Significance

Shri Somnath Temple holds the supreme distinction of being the first (Prathama) of the twelve revered Jyotirlingas of Lord Shiva, celebrated in the ancient Sanskrit shloka: "Saurashtre Somanatham cha Srisaile Mallikarjunam...". Located at Prabhas Patan near Veraval on the western coast of Gujarat, the shrine stands gloriously at the sacred confluence of the three holy rivers Hiran, Kapila, and Saraswati (the Triveni Sangam) meeting the Arabian Sea.

According to the Shiva Purana, Chandra Deva (the Moon God) worshiped Lord Shiva here with deep penance to free himself from Prajapati Daksha's curse of waning. Pleased by Chandra's devotion, Mahadev blessed him to regain his luster periodically, and eternally took abode here as Someshwar ("Lord of the Moon"). Renowned as the "Shrine Eternal", Somnath symbolizes the undying spirit and eternal light of Sanatan Dharma across millennia.
